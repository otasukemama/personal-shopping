The Industrial Tape That Became a Design Icon

September 2026 · 3 min read
Photo placeholder — swap in a real product shot

Most beloved stationery brands start with someone designing something beautiful. mt started with flypaper.

The company behind it, Kamoi Kakoshi, was founded in 1923 in Kurashiki, a small city in Okayama known for its old canal district and denim. For most of its history, it made exactly the kind of thing you'd expect from a hundred-year-old manufacturer: industrial masking tape for painters and factories, plus sticky flypaper — useful, unglamorous, sold in bulk.

Then in 2008, the company took its factory-grade tape-making know-how and pointed it somewhere unexpected: a washi-paper masking tape line called "mt," made for regular people to decorate things with, not mask off a wall before painting it.

Why it caught on

  • The washi (Japanese paper) base tears cleanly by hand — no scissors needed
  • It sticks firmly but peels off without leaving residue or damaging paper underneath, so it can be repositioned and reused
  • Colors and patterns are released constantly, from plain pastels to elaborate prints, which is exactly the kind of thing that turns a useful object into a thing people collect

It's a nice example of a pattern that shows up more than once in Japanese stationery: a company that spent decades perfecting something unglamorous and functional, then found a completely different audience for that same expertise.
