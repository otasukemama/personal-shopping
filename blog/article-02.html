The Pen That Erases Itself

September 2026 · 3 min read
Photo placeholder — swap in a real product shot

Erasable pens aren't a new idea. Most people who've tried one, though, remember the same disappointment: a smudgy ghost of a line left behind, or ink that scratches off the paper along with a bit of the page itself.

Pilot's FriXion pen was built to solve that, and it did it well enough to become one of the quiet giants of the stationery world. It writes like an ordinary gel pen. Flip it over, rub the attached eraser tip across the line, and the ink disappears completely — no smudge, no residue, no torn paper.

The trick isn't the eraser at all. It's the ink. FriXion ink is thermosensitive — it turns invisible at around 65°C, the exact amount of heat generated by friction from the rubber tip. Write, get it wrong, rub it away, write again. It sounds almost too simple, which might be why it took a stationery company that's been perfecting ink since 1918 to actually pull it off.

Why it quietly took over the world

  • First launched in Europe in 2006, then in Japan in 2007 — and it's now sold in over 100 countries
  • Cumulative sales have passed 3.7 billion units, making it one of the best-selling pens in the world
  • Comes in dozens of colors, tip sizes, and body styles, from basic classroom versions to higher-end business models

It's a good reminder that not everything worth shipping from Japan is flashy. Sometimes it's just a pen that does something you didn't think a pen could do.

One thing to know: because the ink is heat-sensitive, writing can fade if left somewhere very hot (like a car dashboard in summer) or become visible again if frozen. Worth keeping in mind for anything you plan to archive long-term.
