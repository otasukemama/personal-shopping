Beerus Was Almost a Pure Villain

September 2026 · 3 min read

Written by our resident anime expert — Sana's son

Photo placeholder — swap in a real photo

Starting this October, Dragon Ball is getting a remastered rebroadcast focused on the Lord Beerus arc. It felt like the perfect excuse to dig into some behind-the-scenes trivia about it.

He was originally written as pure evil

Beerus wasn't always the mostly-harmless, snack-obsessed god of destruction fans know today. In the earliest version of the story, he was a straightforwardly evil villain — the kind of antagonist who infects others with an "evil heart," much like the movie villains that came before him. But production took place not long after the Great East Japan Earthquake, and Akira Toriyama himself suggested changing direction: make it a story that would put a smile on kids' faces instead. That's how Beerus was reborn as the lovable, "not entirely evil" god of destruction he is now.

His name came from a misunderstanding

Beerus, and the names of the characters around him, actually came out of a mix-up. The staff originally proposed his name based on the word "virus." But when Toriyama saw it written out, he misread it as a reference to "beer" instead. Rather than correcting the mistake, the team ran with it — which is why the characters introduced afterward all ended up named after alcoholic drinks, like Whis (from "whiskey") and Champa (from "champagne").

He's designed after Toriyama's own cat

Beerus's look combines the Egyptian god Anubis with something much closer to home: a Cornish Rex, the cat breed Toriyama actually lived with at the time.

Knowing all this might just change how you watch the new broadcast. Enjoy!
