The Planner With a Fandom

September 2026 · 4 min read
Photo placeholder — swap in a real product shot

Most planners are things people buy, use for three weeks, and quietly abandon. The Hobonichi Techo is one of the rare exceptions — a planner with people who wait for it, plan their year around its release date, and swap photos of their filled pages online.

It comes from an unlikely source: not a stationery company, but Hobonichi, the company behind "Hobonichi Itoi Shimbun," a website run by well-known Japanese copywriter Shigesato Itoi. The planner launched in 2001, almost as a side project, and quietly became the company's main product — it now accounts for most of Hobonichi's revenue, and more than half of its sales come from outside Japan, especially the United States.

What makes it different

  • One day, one page. Instead of cramming a week onto two pages, each day gets a full page, with a light grid printed across it so people can write, sketch, or paste things in however they like.
  • Tomoe River paper. The pages are made from an unusually thin, strong paper that resists ink bleed-through, which is part of why it developed a following among fountain pen users specifically.
  • It opens completely flat. The binding lets any page lie flat at 180 degrees, which matters more than it sounds like it should once you're actually trying to write in it.
  • A quote on every page. Each day includes a short line — sometimes a proverb, sometimes something quietly funny — that regular users say they look forward to as much as the planner itself.

It's a good example of something that shows up a lot in Japanese stationery: a very ordinary object treated with a level of care that turns it into something people get genuinely attached to.
